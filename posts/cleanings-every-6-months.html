Why Cleanings Every 6 Months Actually Matter

November 2026 · Dr. Azusa Kaneshima, DDS

Many people come in for a cleaning once a year. The second visit is the one that tends to slip: a busy month, a move, a calendar full of school events. It’s easy to think that if nothing hurts, nothing is wrong. But a lot can quietly build up in six months, and most of it doesn’t hurt at all until it becomes a bigger problem.

What builds up between visits

Every day, a soft, sticky film of bacteria called plaque forms on your teeth. Good brushing and flossing remove most of it, but not all. Plaque that stays behind, especially along the gumline, between teeth and behind the lower front teeth, starts to harden into tartar (also called calculus).

Once tartar forms, a toothbrush can’t remove it. It gives bacteria a rough surface to hold on to, and it keeps growing until it’s removed with professional instruments. That’s the main job of a professional cleaning.

Why it matters, even when nothing hurts

Gum disease starts quietly

Bacteria around the gumline cause inflammation called gingivitis. The signs are easy to miss: gums that look a little red or puffy, or bleed when you brush or floss. Gingivitis can usually be reversed with a cleaning and better home care. Left alone, it can progress to periodontitis, which damages the bone that holds your teeth in place and can’t be fully undone.

Small cavities don’t send warning signs

Early tooth decay usually causes no pain. When we find a cavity while it’s small, it can often be fixed with a simple filling. When it’s found later, after it has reached the nerve, the same tooth may need a root canal and a crown. Regular checkups are how we catch problems at the small, simple stage.

What happens at a checkup and cleaning

  • Professional cleaning: we remove plaque and tartar above and just below the gumline, then polish your teeth.
  • Examination: we check your teeth, gums, bite and any existing fillings or crowns.
  • X-rays when needed: digital X-rays show what we can’t see with our eyes, such as decay between teeth. We have digital X-ray and CT in the clinic.
  • Advice for home: we’ll point out any areas that are easy to miss and suggest simple changes to your routine.

Everything is explained in English, in a fully private treatment room.

Is every six months right for everyone?

Six months is a good starting point for most adults and children. Some people do better with more frequent visits, for example if you have had gum disease before, have diabetes, are pregnant, smoke, or wear braces or clear aligners. Others with very healthy gums may be fine with a longer interval. We’ll recommend the schedule that fits you.

Your insurance probably covers it

Many U.S. dental plans cover two preventive exams and cleanings a year, often at little or no cost to you. If you only come in once a year, you may be leaving a covered visit unused. Coverage varies, so check your plan’s details. We work with TRICARE, United Concordia, Cigna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, MetLife, Delta Dental, Aetna, Aetna International and GEHA. See our insurance FAQ for more.

For kids, regular visits build good habits

Children’s teeth change quickly, and young children often can’t tell you when something feels wrong. Regular checkups help us spot problems early, and they help kids grow up feeling comfortable at the dentist. We see children aged 12 and under for gentle checkups and cleaning.

Between visits

  • Brush twice a day for two minutes with a fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean between your teeth once a day with floss or interdental brushes.
  • Keep sugary snacks and drinks to mealtimes when you can.
  • Don’t wait for your next checkup if your gums bleed often, a tooth becomes sensitive or something feels different. Book an examination.
A cleaning every six months is one of the simplest ways to avoid bigger, more involved treatment later.
